# Spheres: 1990–2035 player workflow QA

**Completed September 8, 2026:** France's continued player campaign and three
fresh daily simulations reached **January 1, 2035**. Terminal accounting and
save-continuity checks pass. Workflow repairs are integrated on GitHub;
long-run economic balance and an intermittent startup failure remain open.

## Scope and provenance

This extends [the 1990–2000 walkthrough](USER_QA_1990_2000.md). The France
player campaign resumes its unchanged January 1, 2000 save and advances with
ordinary game commands. Three independent daily observers start fresh in
1990: USA development, passive Japan, and the Iraq/Kuwait conflict followed
by its long aftermath. The endpoint is January 1, 2035, not the end of 2035.

The baseline is GitHub revision `a14b1230eee1e797246a4cc305ba5710085a0406`.

`754d703af4ef2e252a5f37042bd81bd61c8a582e49da47a2114aec4c7fd8c8d1`.

`artifacts/user-qa-2035/simulation/provenance.json`. Browser initialization

France uses the existing ministry and department shares, renewing its annual
budget normally. No money, political capital, resources, workforce or technology
is inserted into the campaign. Browser interactions cover inspection and

then 90 days, and from 2020 up to 366 days. Requests stop at fiscal boundaries
and honor earlier event interruptions. Every

## Player progression

### 2000–2002: complete the physical supply chain

The carried campaign begins with seven completed projects. France renews its
budget through the browser, then builds Advanced Industry and another Power
Plant in Île-de-France, and a Shipyard, Power Grid and Infrastructure in
Normandie. All twelve projects have completed by January 1, 2002.

Advanced Industry has accumulated 48.110 component packs. Its latest output
is 0.193 packs/day with about 96.6% worker coverage. This is an intermediate
good for later equipment work; it is not a grant of finished equipment.

France opens a Los Angeles-class submarine line at Normandie, stops its old
Light Infantry line, and starts F-15E production in the freed Île-de-France
military factory. Each new line costs the normal eight political capital.
Stopping the old line preserves all 24 already-paid delivery orders exactly.
The submarine and aircraft have nine- and five-year delivery leads respectively.

By 2003, the earlier submarine line consumes the scarce shared components
before the equally prioritized aircraft line. The aircraft's cumulative
ordered value stays at $0.254bn through the January 2005 snapshot. One plant's
maximum 0.2 packs/day cannot cover the roughly 2.989 packs/day of recurring
advanced-line demand reported at that checkpoint. The workflow must help the
player understand priority, supply capacity and delivery time separately.

### 2010–2013: choose what scarce inputs should produce

France reaches 2010 with $1,509.12bn GDP and $4,136.28bn cash, but its prosperity
aim is incomplete. Real income per person is $24,591.90 against the original
fixed $26,735.45 target. The treasury is not a substitute for that objective.

Changing F-15E to high priority through the browser redirects the next day's
scarce components: 0.017853 aircraft equivalents, about $1.607m, enter paid
orders while the submarine line places none. Existing deliveries remain intact.

The player then commits $850m of normally priced construction: a Materials
Plant, Advanced Industry and Military Factory in Normandie, plus a second
Advanced Industry level in Île-de-France. All four finish by 2012, bringing
completed projects to sixteen. Daily construction costs zero political capital
in this active system and pays its bill as work occurs. Its quotes expose
qualified-worker constraints; nominal capacity is not assured usable capacity.

A United States rare-earth contract supplies the new demand for ten years at
$100m/year and three PC to sign. Money settles in proportion to daily dispatches;
goods still need to arrive before they can be used. That minimum diplomatic
price is disproportionately large for the small physical volume and remains
an open balance issue.

The custom-equipment workflow is exercised from design through service:

1. **Horizon APC** uses a deliberately reviewed 900 hp engine. Compared with
   the APC preset, the choice adds 0.1 mobility, $130k per vehicle and $3.9m
   development. The saved revision is fixed when ordered.
2. Development costs $48.84m and takes all 250 required work-days. Certification
   does not create finished vehicles.
3. The first production-site selection correctly refuses an occupied factory.
   Normandie's free military slot is available despite its occupied naval slot.
   Five vehicles cost $16.28m including tooling, with a $100k/day ceiling and
   200 minimum work-days plus delivery. Work cannot begin on the issue date.
4. By January 2013, the batch is complete, fully paid and all five vehicles are
   delivered. The first annual service reading shows 99.2% condition and full
   maintenance funding against a $1,935/day requirement.

In 2012, People shows 197.2k skilled vacancies with about 3.5% unemployment.
The browser's Trade Schools choice costs eight PC and explains its eighteen-month
technical courses. Qualification changes take time; selecting a policy does
not create an instant workforce. Vacancies later fluctuate as demand and the
course pipeline change.

### 2020: review supply before spending again

France holds 85.320 F-15E and 5.977 SSN equipment equivalents, plus the five
custom APC vehicles. The older line system reports equivalents; these are not
claims of fractional physical custom vehicles. The component plants still
constrain both advanced lines.

The rare-earth screen shows 0.05552 kt in stock—9,253 days at current demand.
Its one-year plan needs only 0.00219 kt and has no gap. No replacement import
contract is ordered. This is a clear reason to inspect accumulated inventory
before repeating an earlier action.

Reversing priority to high for SSN and normal for F-15E sends the next day's
$4.752m of paid orders to the submarine line. Aircraft order value remains
$16.740224bn. Priority changes allocation; they do not manufacture extra inputs.

GDP has slipped to $1,439.20bn despite $6,621.76bn cash and zero debt.
Income per person is $22,999.45, still below the prosperity target. These
results preserve the unresolved economic outcome rather than treating a
completed construction queue as a successful prosperity strategy.

### 2030–2035: a functioning economy still needs new decisions

France finishes with **$1,474.668bn GDP, 63.886m people, $10,289.601bn treasury,
zero debt and 179 known technologies**. GDP is 16.12% above the 1990 opening,
but only 2.87% above the carried 2000 save. Real income per person is
$23,082.76, so the original $26,735.45 prosperity aim remains incomplete.

All sixteen construction levels and the five-APC batch are complete. The
standing aircraft and submarine lines are continuing programmes, with physical
input constraints still exposed. The fleet holds 186.002 aircraft equivalents,
16.803 submarine equivalents and five custom APCs. Their respective condition
readings are 76.0%, 88.1% and 51.5%; paying maintenance has not removed age.

The workforce provides the strongest unresolved decision. Unemployment falls
to 0.72%, but skilled vacancies rise to 532.1k and school coverage falls to
51.2%. The smoothed adult graduate pace is about 23.0k/year. Trade Schools
continues, yet its capacity and funding are not enough to eliminate shortages.
The latest component output falls to 0.339 packs/day across three installed
levels, with zero stock. This run holds ministry shares fixed; it is not an
optimized strategy or an isolated experiment attributing GDP to one project.

## Repairs made during this QA

- Manufacturing distinguishes naval and military-factory capacity. The final
  site picker shows the selected equipment type's free slots and the eight-PC
  opening cost. Small equipment prices use readable money units.
- Loaded established lines distinguish missing dated receipts from a first
  production run. Existing saves intentionally omit transient daily receipt
  fields; cumulative orders still survive. The view says "No dated receipt
  available" instead of inventing a date or calling old paid orders new.
- Component help applies to component-using equipment. Ordinary campaigns
  offer the domestic Advanced Industry path; the optional goods-market
  supplier path is reserved for campaigns with that mode enabled.
- Military Factory previews distinguish routine Industry operating costs
  from Defense spending on assigned equipment work. An idle factory is not
  promised to be free to operate.
- Industry snapshots retain current funding and readiness constraints while
  separately displaying dated output, jobs, power and spending receipts.
  Fiscal expiry no longer leaves yesterday's success as today's explanation.
- Valid custom production previews show advanced components required for all remaining
  fabrication, including work after tooling. A zero next-day requirement is no
  longer presented without the future bill. The quote uses the existing
  contractor-adjusted component calculation; it does not reserve stock, change
  consumption or guarantee future supply. Invalid orders retain a raw-material
  fallback until their validation requirements permit a dated component plan.
- Completed campaign aims survive later changes of aim. The bounded history
  still keeps only the newest 24 abandoned attempts, while retaining every
  completed record. Qualification rules and rewards do not change.
- The save menu validates JSON without allocating each entire campaign world
  and history solely to list its metadata. Save writes and actual loading are
  unchanged; malformed/truncated JSON remains unreadable under the old contract.

On the same 20-save, 1.075-GB local fixture, save listing fell from 14.450 to
4.156 seconds for the first request in a fresh process, and from 10.811 to
3.981 seconds for the second request. All 20 returned metadata objects match.
Sampled private memory fell from a roughly 483-MB peak to about 26.4 MB.
Operating-system disk-cache state was uncontrolled; the first request is not
claimed to be a cold-disk benchmark. All file bytes are still validated, so
large directories can still take several seconds.
