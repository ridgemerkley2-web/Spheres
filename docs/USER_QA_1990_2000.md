Prepared 2026-09-08 against integrated revision

**Completed:** France and three independent campaigns reached January 1, 2000,
covering **14,608 daily settlements**, plus separate replay and browser checks.
All four terminal saves passed accounting, exact reload and seven-day

## What this check covers

This combines two different forms of evidence:

- A disposable France campaign started through the browser, with manual
  inspection of construction, supply, logistics, Industry, People, Government
  and narrow-screen navigation. Between inspection points, a small driver
  sends ordinary `/api/advance` requests of at most 30 days, observes the
  returned date and interruptions, renews annual budgets through ordinary
  commands, and records annual API snapshots and named saves. It does not
  edit a save, grant resources, or bypass the daily simulation. This is not
  a claim that every day or every action was clicked manually.
- Three independent observer campaigns on the integrated simulation: Japan
  without player orders, United States development, and an Iraq–Kuwait war
  followed by the remaining decade. Each reached **2000-01-01 after 3,652
  Gregorian daily settlements**, for 10,956 settlements total. They use seed
  1990, normal prices and starting capabilities, and no resource, money,
  political-capital or force grants.

The independent observers enable rebuilt industry, companies, population,
operational warfare, the government lens and physical logistics as fresh
browser games do. Optional Economic Competition stays off. Normal world AI
continues to operate. These few paths do not establish balance across seeds,
historical accuracy, or coverage of every country, weapon, diplomatic choice
and optional mode. Cross-country differences do not isolate a policy's causal
effect.

Raw local evidence is under `artifacts/user-qa-1990-2000/`: `player/` contains
the France action journal and annual snapshots; `simulation/REPORT.md` contains
the full independent walkthrough, supported by `annual-summary.csv`, observer
source/diffs, provenance, dated metrics and terminal saves. These artifact paths
describe local evidence, which may be excluded from Git.

During 1990, delivered inputs allow the first factory to finish. During 1991,
the development base expands to four completed levels: Civilian Factory,
Office District, Power Grid and Materials Plant. They remain present in the
1994 industry reading. Completion is distinct from useful operation: the
factory and offices report worker or qualification limits, and materials
output reports shared power, grid, storage or funding constraints. The player
must address those constraints before assuming another building will help.

Education changes over time. School coverage rises from 65.00% at the opening
snapshot to 69.29% in January 1994. The adult-course graduate reading is zero
in January 1991, then nonzero by January 1992; it is a smoothed annual rate,
not a cumulative count. Living standards reach 102.04 relative to the opening
index of 100. Unemployment varies across years even while buildings and
qualifications grow. Those measurements do not prove that any single order
caused the whole national change.

The 36-month rare-earth contract expires in early January 1993 after the
initial factory has long finished. This demonstrates why a finite input bill
needs to be compared with the entire recurring contract commitment. Annual
budget renewal works with the enrolled department plan. The driver initially
sent the older annual-budget command in January 1992 and received the correct
refusal, then resumed with `program_budget`; the refused attempt remains in
the journal and was not hidden as a game failure or successful action.

Manual Government inspection in mid-1994 showed the 1993 election reflected
in the chamber: Socialist Party control at 62%, approximately 18 months in
office, and the next election in March 1998. Coalition invitations and
party/reform actions disclose prices or locked conditions. The authored
Mitterrand/Rocard identities remain visible; historical replacement was not
tested. **Coup, Uprising, Roundtable and Collapse takeover-watch panels all
say closed, calibration pending.** Those particular takeover paths are
disabled/incomplete, even though elections and other political systems run.

The browser console had no errors or warnings during the reported manual
inspection. At 390 pixels, the construction card and controls remained
readable; the economy navigation uses horizontal scrolling. The rebuilt
binary was loaded with the January 1995 campaign: Industry then truthfully
showed its latest facility operation on January 1, 1995, and Built facilities
showed four of 94 facilities with new cards first. A save/reload audit preserves
the exact world, history and log (`player/reload-1995-audit.json`).

The January 1995 People screen shows approximately 22.7 million employed,
7.3% unemployment, living standards 103, 550,900 recent adult graduates per
year and 70% education access. Trade Schools remains active until a manually
reviewed switch to University Drive, costing eight political capital and
leaving 54. By January 1997, the smoothed adult-graduate rate is 145,716 per
year and living standards are 104.85. These aggregate readings also reflect
eligibility, course timing and changes in employment; the priority is not a
guarantee of more graduates every year.

During 1995, the player adds Machinery Works, a Research Center and a Military
Factory in the developed province. The research construction initially pauses
for platinum. Its card and dock now both report the pause. Ordinary automatic
input purchasing supplies the work without another diplomatic treaty. Machinery
and the center finish by January 1996; the military factory finishes during
1996. Materials output rises from about 1% when storage is full to about 66%
in the January 1996 receipt as new consumers use prepared goods. Machinery
reports 0.5 capital-goods packs per day.

The research center exposes a substantive integration defect: it is staffed
and billed, but its prototype benefit requires the optional Economic Competition
switch even in the default rebuilt-industry campaign. The saved France world
has active research focuses and manufactured inputs, so missing player orders
are not the explanation. This pass repairs the gate; the historical walkthrough
measurements retain the original behavior. Verification of the repaired effect
is recorded separately below.

On January 2, 1997, the player assigns a Light Infantry Formation line to the
new military factory. The slot changes from 0/1 to 1/1; staff procurement is
replaced by the directed line. It quotes $10m per abstract equipment unit and
a 730-day first-delivery lead time. Opening it costs eight political capital,
which was not disclosed on the final province-selection button. That remains
a UI finding. The custom equipment designer was also inspected: the 3D tank
renders, keyboard-accessible part/view controls are present, and development,
tooling, fabrication, maintenance and minimum times are separately quoted.
No custom design or additional procurement order was submitted in that inspection.

During 1997–1999, the player maintains the enacted annual department plan and
lets the completed industrial chain and directed manufacturing line operate.
The queue remains empty. The infantry stock rises by **998.8451 legacy
equipment equivalents** from January 2, 1997 to January 1, 2000. This is net
inventory change, not a count of soldiers or an isolated estimate of deliveries
caused by the new factory. The military-strength index ends at 41.63 versus
44 at the opening; more equipment stock does not guarantee a stronger total
military across every modeled factor.

At January 2000, GDP is **12.8749% above the untouched January 1, 1990 baseline
of $1,270bn**. France has seven completed project levels, **49 additional

zero public debt and $1,688.17bn of treasury cash. Stability is 70.34 versus
75 initially. The very large cash accumulation is a balance observation for
this policy path; the comparison countries do not establish its cause.

## Remaining findings

- **Intermittent automated-browser startup:** one final batch still timed out
  before the campaign opened, despite fixing the undrained response. The
  standard isolated CLI and subsequent diagnostic runs passed. The remaining
  cause is unresolved; manual browser play had no console errors or warnings.
- **Tiny input procurement cost:** the existing minimum diplomatic quote is
  $100m per year, approximately $300m over a full 36-month term. That can be
  disproportionate for a small factory input requirement. The quote is now
  clearer, but this pricing and procurement design issue remains open.
- **Military supply wording:** the USA says “Cut off” on all 32 fielded dates
  in the war trace although its corridor is open and cargo is inbound. The
  first shipment has a booked 42-day route from US-CA to SA-04 and is scheduled
  to arrive after the ceasefire; the trace does not prove delivery occurred.
  “Waiting for first delivery” would distinguish this from a closed
  route or encirclement. Force deployment preparation is a separate pacing
  question; neither rule was changed in this QA pass.
- **National warehouse geography:** France-bound goods can arrive through
  Guadeloupe and become available in the national stockpile. Mainland
  transshipment is abstracted. A real incoming route does not mean the model
  tracks every subsequent domestic leg to the construction province.
- **Facility blocker specificity:** the 1995 Materials Plant shows roughly
  1% usable with a full warehouse, but its generic explanation groups power,
  grid, storage and authority together and still offers Find imports. The
  action should address the specific full-storage constraint. The passive
  Power Grid can also keep saying Awaiting first operation while providing
  its supporting service; it needs wording appropriate to passive capacity.
- **Optional-mode coverage:** the scheduled `daily_calibration` workflow still
  uses legacy default switches for rebuilt industry, companies, population and
  operational warfare. Its 30-year result cannot certify the active integrated
  browser rules. This QA's separate observers use the correct initialization;
  a complete Economic Competition decade is still outside their scope.
- **Political completion:** takeover-watch routes remain closed for calibration.
  Historical leader replacement and the full equipment/procurement catalogue
  were not exhaustively tested.
- **Manufacturing cost disclosure:** opening a directed line costs eight
  political capital, but the final plant-selection button does not show it.
  Small catalogue prices can also round to $0.000bn; use the same readable
  money formatting as construction and the custom equipment designer.
