### How a start and an end are decided

The three checks raised the same questions, and this packet applies one rule to all three parts. A holder has `from` only where
the Constitutional Council, the authority that proclaims the result, states the day the mandate takes effect (its decisions of
2002 and 2022: "à compter du 17 mai 2002 à 0 heure", "à compter du 14 mai 2022 à 0 heure") or, in its President's address at the
investiture, the day office is taken up (2007, 2012, 2017). Those statements are claims dated by the day they were made; the day
they fix is the holder's `from`. A start before 1 January 1990 is not structured: decision 88-60 PDR's "prendra effet le 21 mai
1988 à 0 heure" is a claim, and Mitterrand is dated by `attested_on` (4 January 1990). A holder's own statement that he assumes
office "today" supports a start but never makes one, as CLAUDE-C01-10 ruled, so Chirac's 1995 term is dated only by
`attested_on` (17 May 1995): decision 95-81 PDR proclaims him only "à compter de la cessation des fonctions de M. François
Mitterrand", with an outer limit, and no address of the President of the Council at a 1995 investiture was found. An investiture
ceremony, a proclamation, its publication, a handover meeting and an election round are never boundaries; in 2002 and 2022 the
investiture precedes the stated start.

A holder has `until` only where a source states, as a record, the day the office ended. None does. The Council states only outer
limits ("au plus tard ... à 24 heures", or "à 0 heure" in 1995); Mitterrand (16 May 1995), Chirac (15 May 2007) and Hollande
(10 May 2017) announced their handovers one to four days ahead; Chirac's address of 17 May 1995 speaks of the fourteen years "qui
viennent de s'écouler" without a day; and the Council's undated 2012 note, its 2017 dossier and activity report and the Élysée's
biography timeline are retrospective. The dossiers' proposed and conditional `until` values (Mitterrand 17 May 1995, Chirac
16 May 2007) are therefore withdrawn, following CLAUDE-C01-09 (an end from a same-day statement or an instrument fixing the
effect) and CLAUDE-C01-10 (no prospective ends). No end is inferred from a successor's start: in particular 13 May 2022 is not
the end of Macron's first term.

Three holders' identities rest partly on a vote total. The President of the Council's addresses of 2007, 2012 and 2017 name nobody; each
holder is identified by the second-round total the address prints, which the Council's decision gives for the named candidate,
and each holder note says so. For 2012 the Council's own note also links the address as Jean-Louis Debré's speech "lors de
l'investiture de François Hollande".

Date conventions follow CLAUDE-C01-07, 08 and 09: `attested_on` is the day of the observed event as the source dates it; a
prospective statement is dated by the day it was made, not the day it fixes; a retrospective statement of a single dated event is
dated by the day it states and is never a boundary; rows of retrospective tables and timelines, and an undated link title, carry
no structured date; election rounds held over two days carry a `period`. Élysée archive headings are later catalogue labels:
dates come from the archive's date line, which is marked "Seul le prononcé fait foi".

### The supplement merge

`import_cnccfp_census.py` keeps generating the CNCCFP packet exactly as before and then calls `merge_supplement` (documented in
the module docstring). The supplement has exactly three keys: its `sources` are appended after the eight CNCCFP sources, its
`institutions` become the packet's institutions (the generated packet had none), and its `coverage_unresolved` notes are appended
to the packet's `coverage.unresolved`. The build fails loudly on a missing supplement, invalid JSON, any other key, a malformed
source, claim, institution or note, a source, claim or entry id that collides with the generated packet or within the supplement,
and a snapshot path that is missing or resolves outside `research/sources`. The supplement sits in a subfolder, so
`campaign_research.py`'s non-recursive `research/*.json` glob never treats it as a packet; the merged packet is validated like any
other. Built with an empty supplement, the importer reproduces the previous `france.json` byte for byte (SHA-256
`6ac88df4…4b112c`, pinned by the new test). Codex must accept this mechanism before any later France packet relies on it.

## Observations

### FR-PRES-01 — The President when the period opens

Evidence: decision 88-60 PDR of 11 May 1988 proclaims Monsieur François MITTERRAND and states that his mandate "prendra effet le
21 mai 1988 à 0 heure" (`fr_cc_proclaims_mitterrand_19880511`, `fr_cc_88_60_states_mandate_effect_19880511`); decision 95-81 PDR
cites it in its visas. In his investiture address of 21 May 1988 he speaks of assuming the supreme magistracy "aujourd'hui"
(`fr_mitterrand_assumes_second_septennat_statement_19880521`). In the first week of the period the Élysée archive's transcript of
the Latché press conference of 4 January 1990 has Chancellor Kohl say "Je suis d'accord avec M. Mitterrand" and speak of working
in 1990 with "le Président de la République" (`fr_mitterrand_kohl_press_conference_attestation_19900104`); the 3 January 1990
address is heading-only support. The Assemblée nationale's record of 27 August 1990 reads his decree of 22 August 1990 and his
message of that day, both signed FRANÇOIS MITTERRAND (the first official in-period records found). His reply of 10 May 1995
accepting the Prime Minister's resignation is heading-only support. His letter of 16 May 1995 announces the handover for "Demain
matin à onze heures" (`fr_mitterrand_handover_announced_19950516`).

Decision: accepted in part. The holder is observed on 4 January 1990 (`attested_on`), with no start and no end: the effect day
fixed in advance by the proclaiming authority (21 May 1988) lies before the period and is recorded only as a claim.

Limits: no official record from January to July 1990 was found (the 2 April 1990 sitting record does not name the President); the
Journal officiel of 12 May 1988 was not retrieved; the day his functions ended is not recorded by any source.

### FR-PRES-02 — 1995: the proclamation and Jacques Chirac's assumption of office

Evidence: Mitterrand congratulates M. Jacques Chirac on his election on 7 May 1995 (`fr_mitterrand_congratulates_chirac_election_19950507`).
Decision 95-81 PDR, deliberated 10-12 May and dated **12 May 1995**, proclaims him "à compter de la cessation des fonctions de
M. François Mitterrand", which will occur "au plus tard, le 21 mai 1995 à 0 heure" (`fr_cc_proclaims_chirac_19950512`,
`fr_mitterrand_cessation_latest_bound_19950512`). In his address at the Élysée dated 17 May 1995 he says "En ce jour où je prends la
responsabilité d'assumer la plus haute charge de l'Etat" and "Un nouveau septennat commence"; his message to the armed forces of
the same day reads "Président de la République, c'est en tant que chef des Armées que je m'adresse à vous" and, of the deterrent,
"J'en assume aujourd'hui la responsabilité". The Assembly's record of 19 May reads his letter dated 18 May 1995, signed JACQUES
CHIRAC, whose message refers to "la façon dont s'est opérée la transition d'un septennat à l'autre". The Council's undated 2012
note says the 1995 handover was held on 17 May, and its 2017 dossier table gives the ceremony as 17 May (retrospective).

Decision: accepted in part. The holder is observed on 17 May 1995 with no start (A2): no decision or address of the President of the
Council fixes the day, and his own statements support but never make a start.

Limits: no after-the-fact official record of the 17 May 1995 handover, no 1995 address of the Council's President and no Journal
officiel of 14 May or 18 May 1995 was retrieved.

### FR-PRES-03 — 2002: the proclamation and the start of Chirac's second term

Evidence: the election-night declaration of 5 May 2002, which the Élysée archive copied from the campaign site on 6 May
