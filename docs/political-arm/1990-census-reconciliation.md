# January 1990 ruling-bloc census reconciliation

Decision record, 22 September 2026. This changes the P-6 **static data contract**;
it does not change any A1–A10 simulation acceptance threshold.

The opening census must follow the named governments on **1 January 1990**.
The original design estimate of 11–13 Communist-bloc governments cannot be an
acceptance quota for the existing party-family definition: the transcribed
roster already contained 17. That original check failed, and those results remain
in the archived September 22 verification evidence. It is now replaced by an
active, exact named-roster assertion, alongside the full five-bloc census and
the unchanged Iran/Sudan, Libya, Poland and other country checks. The old range
has been superseded explicitly; it has not been met by changing labels to fit it.

Two additional opening-date errors were corrected in `leaders_1990.json`:

| Government | Opening bloc | Evidence and date distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Angola, José Eduardo dos Santos / MPLA-PT | Communist | The contemporary [US Department of State 1990 report](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1119700.html), opening paragraphs, identifies the governing party as Marxist-Leninist. The later multiparty MPLA election row keeps its social-democratic family. |
| São Tomé and Príncipe, Manuel Pinto da Costa / MLSTP | Communist | The country's own [CEDAW/C/STP/1-5 report](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/3956850/files/CEDAW_C_STP_1-5-EN.pdf), paragraphs 143–144, describes the 1975–1990 political system as Marxist. [IPU's 1991 election report](https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/HTML/2275_91.htm) dates the multiparty referendum to August 1990 and the later MLSTP-PSD result to January 1991. That dormant election row keeps its social-democratic family. |

These are explicit opening-leader overrides. They do not recode all socialist
parties as Communist, alter institutional pillar affiliations, or replay
historical transitions after the campaign diverges.

The resulting named Communist-bloc roster is Afghanistan, Albania, Angola,
Bulgaria, Cambodia, China, Congo, Cuba, Ethiopia, Laos, Madagascar, Mongolia,
Nicaragua, North Korea, São Tomé and Príncipe, Seychelles, USSR, Vietnam and
Yugoslavia. The other 17 entries retain their transcribed party-family mappings
and existing source trails; this repair does not claim a new independent audit
of every historical observation in those countries.

The five-bloc census is now Western **65**, Communist **19**, Nationalist **9**,
Islamist **2**, Non-Aligned **42**, across 137 opening countries. The game's
bloc categories are coarse design mappings, not claims that all members had
identical institutions or belonged to the same diplomatic alliance.

The original disputed extra entries cannot simply be removed to restore a
quota. For example, [IPU's Nicaragua 1990 election report](https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/HTML/2235_90.htm)
places the FSLN's defeat after the January start, and the contemporary
[US State Department Yugoslavia report](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1257185.html)
records the governing League of Communists at the end of 1989. Seychelles'
socialist SPPF was still the sole governing party before the later transition,
as recorded by [IPU's 1993 election account](https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/HTML/2279_93.htm).

Future source corrections must identify the country, governing organization,
date, applicable game mapping and effect on the named roster. An aggregate
count by itself is insufficient evidence for a historical-data change.
