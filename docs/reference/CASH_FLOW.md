# National cash flow

Open **Economy → The big picture** to see the government's finances together.
The overview shows current treasury cash and debt, the last recorded daily
government budget settlement, the annual funding plan and construction funding.

The daily settlement reports revenue, ministry spending and debt interest for
its displayed date. Services, industry and research operations, construction,
and other capital purchases are parts of ministry spending. They are not extra
bills on top of it. Equipment paid for earlier is shown separately and is never
charged a second time. A missing settlement means no complete daily record is
available; it does not mean the government collected or spent zero.

**Financial priorities** highlights up to three things to review, with the
amounts and dates behind each explanation. Opening or renewing the annual plan
and restoring paused construction funding come first. A recorded deficit shows
the balance before interest and the final result. The largest ministry payment
links to that ministry's funding controls; it includes the ministry's share of
pooled construction payments and is not a recommendation to cut its services.
An annual full-use gap is labelled as an estimate, separate from daily spending.

Each priority opens the relevant controls without changing your plan. You can
also use **Review [ministry]** in the expanded annual table to jump directly to
that ministry. Your queued budget and policy changes remain in the draft.

The annual plan is an estimate at the current economic and policy readings.
Its full-use comparison assumes every authorized ministry dollar is spent.
This can show a funding gap even when the latest day had a surplus because
unused construction and capital funding is not automatically spent. The annual
figures are not a prediction of the year's final cash balance. Draft budget and
policy changes remain separate until enacted.

Construction shows the applied daily limit, planned next work and available
capital funding. That funding is permission to spend from the annual plan, not
a separate treasury cash account. Raising the daily limit does not increase the
annual budget. Projects can have enough authorization to proceed while the
national plan relies on cash or borrowing. Unused authorization expires at the
fiscal-year boundary; unfinished work keeps its progress.

Use the overview's funding explanations and shortcuts to review the annual
budget, taxes and interest, construction, or operating industry. The construction
manager also has **Review national cash flow**, including while reviewing a new
project's effects. Navigation does not enact a budget or place a project.

Current treasury and debt balances can also reflect trade, transfers and other
transactions. The displayed daily budget settlement covers government revenue,
ministry expenses and interest; it is not a complete journal of every cash
movement. A budget surplus reduces debt before building treasury cash; a deficit
uses available cash before adding debt. The overview does not invent cash runway,
investment returns, sales, tax gains or GDP bonuses.
