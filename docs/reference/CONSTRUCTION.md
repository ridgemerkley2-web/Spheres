# Construction funding

Open **Economy → Construction**, enter a daily budget in millions of dollars,
and apply it. Add a project and choose an owned province to review its effects.
The review compares local facilities, output or freight capacity and the
country's resulting capacity, supply and financial commitments. It also shows
the contract price, minimum installation time, current funding estimate and
operating inputs after completion. Choose **Add to construction queue** only
when you are ready to start; choosing a province spends nothing.

Search provinces by name or code when placing a project. Each result shows its
existing upgrades, workshop capacity and active projects. **Show unavailable
provinces** explains missing site or research requirements. Choosing a workshop
location keeps the national queue visible; **Build here** keeps an explicit
province scope until you return to all national projects.

The Economy overview links to your current yearly plan, construction and the
**Advisor**. Advice prioritizes funding problems, then uses the same suggested
projects and impact reviews. Economic details remain available in an expandable
section below the next step.

The same overview now brings together treasury cash, debt, the latest dated
government-budget settlement and the annual funding plan. **Review national
cash flow** in the construction manager opens this view before you commit to a
project. Planned construction draws from the existing daily limit and annual
capital funding; it is not an extra bill added to the whole ministry envelope.
See [National cash flow](CASH_FLOW.md) for the meaning of each period and balance.

After starting, watch the project's spending, remaining bill, progress and
estimated completion in the same queue.
Smaller starter workshops and mapped mine development are available from this
desk too. Mines appear in the queue alongside buildings.

Open **Economy → Industry** to follow completed facilities. Plants run
automatically when operating funds, inputs and power are available. Each plant
shows its last recorded output, spending and constraint with the receipt date.
Facilities without a receipt show **Awaiting first operation**; supporting
assets explain their role instead of appearing to be failed producers.

Use **Needs attention** to find constrained operations, or search by province
and facility name. **Fund operations** and **Fund energy** open the relevant
ministry department; raw supplies, research and equipment have direct links to
their existing controls. Stock is inventory, not treasury cash, and recorded
output is not a forecast. This screen works without Economic Competition.
Construction still in progress appears below the completed facilities. Finished
mines remain in Resources. The national economy link shows the broader inherited
economy and project value added.

**Suggested construction** highlights useful next projects and explains the
country need and proposed location. Suggestions count existing and queued
capacity so an upgrade already under construction can cover the need. They
distinguish current bottlenecks from opportunities to develop new industry.
Choose **Review suggestion** to inspect the same full impact preview; a
suggestion never places an order or changes your budget automatically.

The same review applies to workshop sizes and mine deposits. These figures use
the current simulation and assume the facility is completed. Rated output still
needs operating funds, inputs and power. The review identifies prerequisites
and bottlenecks, including freight routes limited by the neighboring province.
It does not forecast guaranteed sales, profit, tax receipts or GDP growth.
Changing the date, campaign, budget or selected project refreshes the decision
before another order can be confirmed.

The budget is a ceiling on actual work payments. A $200 million project receiving
$250,000 per day takes about 800 days. More funding shortens that estimate until
the site's installation limit is reached. Funding can then support additional
sites. Higher-priority building projects receive funding first, followed by
other buildings and mines. The estimate reflects today's funding and queue.

Setting the budget to zero pauses construction. Finished work stays in place,
and no construction bill is charged for that day. Canceling a building stops
future bills; completed work is not refunded. Unused daily funding is never
charged. Construction does not require political capital, material stockpiles
or a national construction-capacity score.

The daily budget uses available civilian capital funding from the annual fiscal
plan. It does not add money to that plan. Increase capital allocations in the
annual budget if this funding is exhausted. Equipment procurement and service
funding remain reserved for their own purposes. Actual bills reach the treasury
once through the existing fiscal settlement, using cash and then borrowing.

Factories still need operating inputs and power after they open. An industrial
estate enables processing and machinery sites; some upgrades require existing
facilities or research. Ownership, contested territory, province limits and
minimum installation times remain meaningful.

Existing daily saves retain paid progress, historical material receipts and
purchased workshop sizes. Old prepaid mine projects retain their paid schedule.
Legacy monthly simulation keeps its previous construction behavior.

Project prices are game estimates for complete installations. They do not claim
to be historical construction bids or sourced provincial capital valuations.
