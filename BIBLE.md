# SPHERES — the bible

*What this game is, what it refuses to be, and how to decide when they conflict.*

**Status: draft for Ridge to edit.** Everything in section 9 is an open question
only he can answer. Everything else is settled unless he says otherwise.

Read order: this file, then SPEC.md (technical design), then PLAN.md (sequence),
then ROADMAP.md (status), then CLAUDE.md (how to work).

---

## 1. The one sentence

**SPHERES is the modern-day grand strategy game where nothing is authored:
national capability is derived from real data through a real model, every crisis
is generated by incentives rather than fired by a date, and war is a decision
about how much force to commit rather than a WW2 front line wearing modern
sprites.**

The target is explicit: **a better version of HOI4 Millennium Dawn.**

## 2. The wedge

**Authored content is finite; modelled content is not.** Every complaint MD's
own players make is downstream of that one fact.

Millennium Dawn is not an engine. It is roughly a decade of Paradox script
bolted onto a wargame built to simulate industrial total war in 1936. The split
inside it is almost total, and it is the most important fact in this document:

- Everything MD is *famous* for — GDP, budgets, taxation, debt, interest rates,
  inflation, influence spheres, the UN and EU, internal factions — lives in a
  scripted layer that touches the simulation only through modifier plumbing.
- Everything MD is *deep* in — the province map, division templates, equipment,
  doctrines, combat resolution, supply, air, navy, intelligence — is stock HOI4
  that MD only reskins.

MD's genuine inventions are all in the layer SPHERES already does better with a
real model. MD's operational depth is entirely borrowed machinery we are free to
design from scratch instead of imitating. **We are strong exactly where it is
weakest and absent exactly where it is strongest.**

Concretely: Russia's 45 civilian factories in MD's 2000 start is a number a
contributor typed into a state file — a contestable political claim. Here it is
derived. And MD's history runs out at year 10–15 when the focus trees are
exhausted and the world goes quiet; ours cannot run out, because nothing was
authored to be used up.

## 3. What we keep from MD — non-negotiable

These are why 1.05M people subscribe to a mod its own community calls flawed.
Lose any of them and we have built a different product, not a better one.

1. **Playing the present with real names.** Real leaders, parties, institutions,
   borders, equipment designations. A beautiful model of anonymous nations is
   not a competitor to MD. Every derived quantity still needs a real name on it.
2. **Being allowed not to fight.** MD players say this repeatedly: you can be
   totally pacific and still matter. A full campaign with zero wars must be a
   complete, satisfying playthrough. **The war layer must never become the
   content engine.** This is the strongest fit with what SPHERES already is.
3. **Total world coverage.** MD players pick Kazakhstan, Djibouti, Bosnia. A
   24-nation game does not read as a modern-day game at any level of internal
   quality — it reads as a tech demo. Cutting to 60 is right on engineering
   grounds and wrong on product grounds.

## 4. What we replace, and with what

| MD system | Our replacement | Why it is better |
|---|---|---|
| Factory counts as economy | The macroeconomy that already exists — TFP, capital, inflation, debt, oil, embargo | MD cannot express a currency crisis or a sanctions regime because HOI4 has no prices |
| National focus trees | Emergent history from incentives, plus doctrine gates and a stratagem deck | Trees are rails and they run out; incentives do not |
| Party popularity slider | Political capital as a real budget, coalitions, and legitimacy earned by delivery | Governing becomes a constraint rather than a colour |
| Division templates and fronts | Theatres, a commitment ladder, and force packages (§6) | Modern war is a capability gate, not symmetric attrition |
| Diplomacy as HOI4 factions | Spheres of influence: patronage, pacts, dependency, covert action, access | The namesake system, and the thing MD's audience most wants deepened |

## 5. What we refuse

The most useful section in this document. When a decision is ambiguous, check
here first.

- **No province-level tactical map.** No divisions, no templates, no combat
  width, no encirclement, no front-line drawing. This is the single largest cost
  in any HOI4-parity plan, and it buys a model of industrial attrition that is
  *wrong for the period*. The map carries roughly 1,500 admin-1 districts as
  **political** geography, never 13,000 provinces as tactical geography.
- **No production minigame.** No equipment lines, no factory placement, no build
  queue. Modern platforms take 10–25 years from requirement to fielding and
  serve for 40. You cannot out-produce a war, so a production queue models
  nothing real. Named equipment is *data attached to tech nodes*; nobody places
  a factory.
- **No focus trees, ever.** No scripted event chains beyond physical facts like
  proliferation test dates. This is an accepted **product risk**, not an
  oversight: a large share of MD's audience believes focus trees *are* the game.
  Doctrine gates and the stratagem deck are the deliberate replacement. If they
  do not land, the answer is to make them better — never to start authoring
  trees.
- **No hourly tick.** Combat resolves in closed form monthly, with attrition
  integrated analytically so its expectation matches a continuous process. A
  variable-resolution clock would mean auditing every `/12.0` in the codebase,
  recalibrating every emergent-history test, and risking the determinism
  invariant — for fidelity nobody at this altitude can perceive.
- **No naval task forces or ship-level detail.** Sea power enters as lift
  capacity, access and standoff strike.

## 6. The war model

**Thesis: HOI4 models war as symmetric attrition with quality as a multiplier.
Modern war is a capability gate — either you can find and kill them faster than
they can react, or you cannot, and those are two different equations rather than
two settings of one.** Build the gate and both Desert Storm and Fallujah fall
out of the same model with no hand-tuned numbers.

**The player never commands a unit.** Per theatre they set an objective (deny,
degrade, seize, hold, stabilise, withdraw), a force allocation, rules of
engagement, an escalation ceiling, and an acceptable-casualties threshold that
auto-terminates. Command by intent, at the altitude of national command. At a
monthly tick across ~190 nations it is also the only viable interaction model.

**Four objects:**

1. **Conflict** replaces `War`. Persistent, between coalitions, carrying the
   contested districts, each belligerent's independently chosen commitment rung,
   each side's resolve, a shared escalation risk, and a duration class it slides
   between: conventional, irregular, frozen. Conflicts do not begin with a
   declaration or end with a capitulation. They begin when someone climbs, and
   end when a track collapses.

2. **The commitment ladder**, nine rungs: rhetoric → sanctions → arms to a proxy
   → advisers and intelligence → deniable forces → standoff strike → blockade or
   limited incursion → full conventional campaign → occupation. Each side picks
   its own rung monthly. Escalating costs political capital priced by regime
   type; de-escalating costs reputation. **Mismatched rungs are the interesting
   state**: you at 8 and them at 4 is Afghanistan 2002–21; you at 6 and them at
   8 is Kosovo 1999; both at 2 is the entire Cold War periphery. SPHERES already
   has rungs 2, 3, 4 and 5 built as separate systems — the ladder is what binds
   them into a sequence.

3. **Theatre**: a named operating area over districts, carrying distance from
   each belligerent's nearest sustaining base, terrain, urbanisation, population,
   and an **access requirement** — which third states must consent to basing,
   transit and overflight. Access is a diplomatic quantity, not a supply formula.
   No power commits above roughly rung 5 without a consenting host in range.
   Turkey's parliament refusing transit in 2003 reshaped an invasion, and HOI4
   has no mechanism for a non-belligerent ally vetoing your logistics. This
   single feature turns statecraft into a direct military input and gives small
   states real agency in great-power wars.

4. **Force package**, replacing the `mil_strength` scalar with three stocks on
   deliberately mismatched time constants:
   - **Force structure** — trained people and platforms. Decades to build,
     impossible to surge. Carries a **deployable fraction**: what share can be
     abroad and sustained at once. The US could project ~15%; Iraq in 1990
     almost nothing past its border. *This one number does more work than
     anything else in the model, because it is the actual difference between a
     large army and a power.*
   - **Munitions** — drains in weeks of high intensity, rebuilds slowly. This is
     what makes short wars end for logistical rather than political reasons.
   - **Resolve** — the political stock that actually loses modern wars.

## 7. The emergence contract

What replaces focus trees, stated as a promise we can be held to:

- Every major real event of the period must be **reachable**, and the important
  ones must occur in *most* seeds when nobody intervenes. Never all.
- No event fires on a date. The only exceptions are physical facts — nuclear
  test dates — and they are listed exhaustively in SPEC.
- A player who intervenes must be able to prevent any of it.
- Validation is emergent-history tests across seeds, and a threshold stated once
  rather than chosen ad hoc per test.
- **The tell that we are drifting:** if a system needs a special case named after
  a country, the model is wrong. Yugoslavia's breakup is driven by inherited
  ethnic arithmetic, not by a `if id == Yugoslavia` branch.

## 8. Fidelity contract

- **Transcribed, never invented**: 1990 starting data, real names, real dates.
- **Modelled, may be stylised**: growth rates, war outcomes, political shifts.
  A major economy's 35-year trajectory should land within a stated band of
  reality; a minor one is allowed to be wrong.
- **Correctness beats feel.** When a mechanic is economically wrong but makes
  the world livelier, it goes. Trade agreements paid a permanent growth rate and
  made the world feel dynamic; they were wrong and they were fixed.
- **The smell test for a hidden bug:** if a nation's GDP series and its growth
  series disagree, something is writing to GDP directly.

## 9. Open questions — Ridge decides

*Answer these and the rest of the document becomes actionable. Dated 10 Aug 2026.*

1. **Roster: 190 or 120?** 190 is months of transcription, parallelisable across
   agent sessions. 60 gets most of the texture for a third of the cost but loses
   "any country on Earth", which is part of MD's fantasy. **Answer with a number.**
2. **Start date: 1990 only, or add a 2000 bookmark?** 1990 is a genuine
   differentiator — MD ships only 2000, and the Soviet collapse, reunification
   and the Yugoslav wars are our best emergent-history showcase. But 2000 is the
   ground MD's audience stands on. *Recommendation: ship 1990 as flagship, add
   2000 later as a data file, never as a second codebase.*
3. **Materiel fidelity: in or out?** Named real equipment on tech nodes buys
   enormous credibility with the slice of the audience that is there for the
   hardware. It also creates a permanent maintenance tax and constant pull
   toward simulating platforms. *Are you willing to ship names without
   simulation and defend that line?*
4. **Will you say "no focus trees, ever" out loud and defend it?** If not, you
   will end up authoring trees under pressure at year two, and that is the
   failure mode that turns a model into a content mill.
5. **Do you accept killing PLAN.md Phase 4 as written?** Your own plan lists the
   HOI4-style operational layer as cut #1 under time pressure, and you have just
   committed to a target whose defining feature is that layer. *Recommendation:
   delete Phase 4.2 as specified; replace with §6 above.*
6. **Two years or five?** Roughly 24–30 months of sustained work with heavy agent
   assistance to something that genuinely beats MD, with a playable artifact
   beating it on every axis MD players actually praise at around 15–18 months.
   Allowing for life, 3–4 calendar years to 1.0. The HOI4-clone path is 4–6.
